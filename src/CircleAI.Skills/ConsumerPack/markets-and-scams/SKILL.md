# Money, markets and avoiding scams

You do not need to follow the markets to be good with money — but understanding a
few things protects you from losing what you have.

## What the "markets" are, plainly

- The **JSE** is where shares in big companies are bought and sold; their prices
  rise and fall with how the companies and the economy do.
- **Inflation** is prices going up over time — it quietly shrinks money kept under
  the mattress, which is why saving in an account that pays interest matters.
- **Interest rates** set by the Reserve Bank change what loans cost and what
  savings earn. When rates rise, debt gets more expensive — a reason to pay it
  down. See *Getting out of debt*.

## Saving and investing simply

- **First: an emergency cushion** in a normal savings account you can reach — before
  any investing.
- **Investing** (shares, unit trusts, a retirement product) is for money you will
  not need for **years**. It goes up and down; over long periods it has tended to
  beat inflation, but there are no guarantees.
- Use **regulated providers** only — a licensed bank, a registered financial
  services provider (FSP). You can check a provider with the **FSCA**.
- **Fees eat returns** — ask what you are being charged.

## Spot the scam — this is the important part

Ordinary people are targeted by "investments" that are really theft. Walk away if
you see:

- **Guaranteed high returns** ("double your money", "30% a month"). Real
  investment is never guaranteed, and high return always means high risk.
- **Pay to join and earn by recruiting others** — that is a pyramid/Ponzi scheme;
  it always collapses and the last in lose everything.
- **Pressure to act now**, secrecy, or "only for people you bring in".
- Anyone who is **not a registered FSP** but wants your money to invest.

If it sounds too good to be true, it is. Check the provider with the **FSCA**
before you pay a cent.
