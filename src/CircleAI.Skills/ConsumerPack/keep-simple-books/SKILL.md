# Keep simple books

Books are just a record of **money in** and **money out**. Kept weekly, they
tell you if you are making money, and they make tax and loans easy. A phone note
or a cheap cash book is enough to start.

## The one habit that matters

Every day or every week, write down:

- **Money in** — date, who paid, how much, what for.
- **Money out** — date, who you paid, how much, what for.
- Keep the **slip or proof** for each. A photo of the receipt is fine.

Money in minus money out over a month is your **profit**. If it is negative,
your price is too low or your costs are too high.

## Writing an invoice

An invoice asks a customer to pay. Put on it:

1. **Your name / business name** and contact number.
2. The word **Invoice** and a **number** (start at 001 and count up).
3. The **date**.
4. **What you did or sold**, and the **amount**. List each item.
5. **How to pay** — your bank details or cash — and by **when**.
6. If you are registered for VAT, show your **VAT number** and the VAT amount.

A **quote** is the same thing before the work, saying what it will cost. Turn the
quote into an invoice once the job is done.

## Why it is worth it

- **SARS** wants records if you are registered — good books mean no scramble.
- A bank or lender will ask to see them before a loan.
- You catch a customer who has not paid.
