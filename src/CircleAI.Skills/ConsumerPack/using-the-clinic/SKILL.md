# Using the clinic and when to get help

Public clinics are **free** for South African citizens and permanent residents.
This helps you know **where to go** — it is not a diagnosis. If you are unsure, go
to the clinic; they would rather see you.

## What's free at the clinic

- Check‑ups and basic medicines
- **Baby and child immunisations** (the shots on the Road‑to‑Health card) and growth
  checks
- **Pregnancy (antenatal) care** and family planning
- **HIV testing and treatment** (ARVs), and **TB testing and treatment**
- Ongoing medicine for **high blood pressure, diabetes and epilepsy**
- Basic mental‑health and dental help

## Clinic or hospital?

- For everyday care, **start at your nearest clinic.**
- To see a hospital for something non‑urgent, you usually need a **referral letter**
  from the clinic first.
- In an **emergency, go straight to the nearest hospital casualty or call an
  ambulance** — don't wait for a referral.

## Get help NOW — call 10177 (ambulance) or 112 from any cellphone

Go straight to hospital / call for help if you or someone has:
- chest pain, or trouble breathing
- heavy bleeding that won't stop
- signs of a **stroke** — face drooping, one arm weak, speech slurred
- a **fit/seizure** that won't stop, or won't wake up afterwards
- a **baby or child who won't wake, won't feed, is floppy, or is breathing fast**
- a bad burn, a bad injury, or swallowed poison

## Take with you

Your ID or clinic card, any medicine you're taking, and for a child the
**Road‑to‑Health (clinic) card.**

*Tell me what's wrong and I'll help you decide whether it's a clinic visit or you
should get help right away.*
