# Managing a long-term health condition

Conditions like **HIV, TB, diabetes (sugar) and high blood pressure** cannot
always be cured, but they can be controlled — and people live full, long lives
with them. The treatment for these is **free at government clinics**.

## The one rule: take your treatment every day

- **Take your medicine exactly as told, every day, even when you feel fine.**
  Feeling better does not mean it is gone — stopping is how it comes back, often
  worse and harder to treat (this is how TB and HIV become resistant).
- If side effects bother you, **do not just stop** — tell the clinic; they can
  help or change it.
- Never run out. Collect your repeat **before** the last few pills.

## Work with your clinic

- Go to your **check-ups** even when you feel well — they catch problems early.
- Many clinics let stable patients collect repeats quickly through a **pick-up
  point or chronic club** instead of queueing all day. Ask about it.
- Keep a **clinic card / your numbers** (blood pressure, sugar, viral load) so you
  and any nurse can see how you are doing.

## Live in a way that helps

- **HIV:** taking ARVs properly can make the virus **undetectable**, which means
  you stay healthy and cannot pass it on to a partner. Testing is free.
- **TB:** finish the **full course** — this is the whole treatment, not until you
  feel better. Cover your cough; get close contacts tested.
- **Diabetes and blood pressure:** less salt, less sugar, more vegetables,
  moving daily, and no smoking all help the medicine work.

## Get help fast if

You have chest pain, trouble breathing, confusion, a very high sugar reading, or
you are coughing blood — go to the clinic or **call an ambulance 10177 / 112**.

*I am not a doctor — your clinic gives the medical advice. Tell me your condition
and I will help you keep track of your medicine, appointments and questions to
ask the nurse.*
