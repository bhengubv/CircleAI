# Farming crops and livestock

Whether it is a few goats and a maize patch or a plot you want to sell from, the
same things decide success: **healthy soil, enough water, healthy animals, and a
buyer.**

## Crops

- **Plant for your rainfall and season** — plant with the rains, choose what
  grows in your area. Your local **extension officer** or co-op knows what works.
- **Feed the soil** — compost, kraal manure, and resting or rotating land keep it
  producing. Soil that is never fed stops giving.
- **Water wisely** — mulch to hold moisture, water early or late to lose less to
  the sun.
- Grow some **staples to eat** and a **cash crop to sell** so a bad price does not
  leave you with nothing.

## Livestock

- **Water, grazing/feed, and shelter** are the daily basics. Do not keep more
  animals than your land and feed can carry — thin animals sell for little.
- **Dip and vaccinate** on your area's programme; treat for worms and ticks. See
  *Looking after your animals*.
- **Report sudden deaths or many sick animals** to the state vet — some diseases
  must be reported by law and can wipe out a herd.

## Selling what you grow

- Know your **buyer before harvest** — a local market, a bakkie trader, a shop, a
  co-op. Fresh produce with no buyer rots.
- **Group up** with other small farmers to sell in bigger, better-priced lots and
  share transport.
- Add value where you can — sorted, cleaned, or processed produce earns more.

## Get support

- Your **Department of Agriculture extension officer** gives free advice and can
  point you to input schemes and training for small farmers.
- Farming grants and support exist for smallholders — ask the department; be wary
  of anyone charging a fee to "get you a farming grant".

what to plant, how to keep your animals healthy, and where to sell.*
