# Renting a home — your rights

If you rent a place in South Africa, the **Rental Housing Act** protects you — even
if your lease is only a verbal agreement (though always try to get it in writing).
This is general information, not legal advice; for a specific problem the **Rental
Housing Tribunal** in your province helps free, or call **Legal Aid on 0800 110 110**.

## Your deposit

- The landlord must keep your deposit in a bank account that earns **interest — and
  that interest is yours.**
- When you move out and the place is in good order, you get your **deposit back with
  the interest.**
- Money can only be taken off for **real damage you caused** (not normal wear like
  faded paint), and the landlord must show you **itemised receipts** for it.
- Always get a **receipt** for your deposit and for every rent payment.

## Inspections — do them together

- Walk through the place **with the landlord at move‑in and again at move‑out**, and
  write down the condition. Take photos.
- If the landlord skips the move‑out inspection with you, the law treats the place as
  fine — and they owe you the **full deposit back.**

## They cannot just throw you out

- A landlord **may not lock you out, cut your water or electricity, or remove your
  things.** That is illegal.
- You can only be evicted by a **court order**, and only the **sheriff of the court**
  may carry it out. You have the right to go to court and be heard first.

## If something goes wrong

- Take your complaint to the **Rental Housing Tribunal** (free) — deposits, unfair
  rent, bad repairs, or being locked out.
- Keep your lease, receipts, photos and messages — they are your proof.
