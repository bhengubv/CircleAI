# Feeding your family well

Eating well doesn't have to cost a lot. This is general nutrition, not medical advice;
your **clinic checks your child's growth for free**.

## Eating well on a little

- Build meals around cheap staples — **maize meal (pap), samp, rice, potatoes, bread**
  — then add a **vegetable** (cabbage, spinach/morogo, carrots, butternut) and a
  **protein** when you can.
- **Beans, lentils and eggs** are cheap, filling protein. **Beans with pap** is a
  complete, affordable meal. Tinned fish (pilchards) is good value too.
- Buy the big bag with a **stokvel** to save; **cook once, eat twice.**
- Cut back on sugary cooldrink and chips — they cost money and fill without feeding.

## Babies and small children

- For the **first 6 months, breast milk only** is best — it's free and protects the
  baby. If you use formula, mix it **exactly as the tin says** with clean, **boiled
  (then cooled) water.**
- From **6 months**, add soft foods (mashed veg, pap, mashed beans, mashed egg) and
  keep breastfeeding.
- Take the child to the clinic for **free growth checks, vitamin A and deworming**, and
  keep the **Road‑to‑Health card** up to date.

## See the clinic if a child

- is **very thin, or swollen** in the feet/face, or **not growing**
- **won't eat**, or has **diarrhoea/vomiting** with **few wet nappies** (dehydration)
