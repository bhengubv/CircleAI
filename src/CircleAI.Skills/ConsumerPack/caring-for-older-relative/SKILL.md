# Caring for an older relative

Caring for a parent or grandparent is an act of love and it is hard work. You do
not have to do all of it, or pay for all of it, alone.

## Money: the older person's grant

- **SASSA** pays an **Older Person's Grant** (the state pension) from age 60 to
  people whose income and assets are below the limit. Apply at your nearest SASSA
  office with the person's ID, and proof of income and residence. See *Apply for
  a social grant*.
- Someone very frail who needs full-time care may qualify for a **Grant-in-Aid**
  on top — ask SASSA.

## Health

- Their **clinic care and chronic medicine are free**. Help them collect repeats
  on time — see *Managing a long-term health condition*.
- Go with them to appointments if you can; two people remember more than one.
- Watch for new confusion, falls, not eating, or a sudden change — tell the
  clinic; these are not just "old age".

## Safety at home

- Clear loose mats and cords, add a light for night-time trips to the toilet, and
  put a grab-hold near the bath — **falls** are the biggest home danger.
- Keep their **medicines in order** so doses are not missed or doubled.
- Keep emergency numbers where they can see them and make sure they can reach a
  phone. **Ambulance 10177 / 112.**

## Look after the carer too

- **You cannot pour from an empty cup.** Ask family to share the load — set a
  rota — and take breaks.
- Elder abuse and neglect are real; if an older person is being harmed or their
  grant is being taken, that is a crime — call the **GBV / abuse line 0800 428
  428** or the police **10111**.

*Tell me about the person you are caring for and what is hardest right now, and I
will help you find the grant, the clinic support, or a way to share the load.*
