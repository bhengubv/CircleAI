# Your rights at work

Most workers in South Africa are protected by the **Basic Conditions of
Employment Act (BCEA)**, whether or not you signed a contract. These are the
basics. This is general information, not legal advice — for a specific problem
contact the **Department of Employment and Labour** or the **CCMA** (both free).

## Leave

- **Annual leave:** 21 consecutive days of paid leave for every 12 months you
  work (that is the same as one day of leave for every 17 days worked).
- **Sick leave:** over a 36-month cycle you get the number of days you normally
  work in six weeks (usually 30 days on a five-day week). In your first six
  months, you get one paid sick day for every 26 days worked.
- **Family responsibility leave:** paid days for things like the birth of your
  child or the death of a close family member, if you have worked there more
  than four months.
- **Maternity leave:** at least four consecutive months (you can claim from UIF).

## Hours and pay

- Ordinary time is usually up to **45 hours a week**. Overtime is by agreement
  and must be paid extra (normally 1.5 times your rate).
- You must get a **payslip** showing your pay and what was taken off.
- There is a **national minimum wage** — check the current rate; your employer
  may not pay less.

## Notice if the job ends

Either side must give notice:
- **One week** if you have worked there six months or less.
- **Two weeks** if between six months and one year.
- **Four weeks** if one year or more (also farm and domestic workers after six
  months).

## UIF

Your employer must register you for **UIF** and pay contributions. If you lose
your job, or go on maternity leave, you can claim from the UIF.

## If something is wrong

Keep your contract, payslips and any messages. You can get free help from the
Department of Employment and Labour or refer a dispute to the CCMA.
