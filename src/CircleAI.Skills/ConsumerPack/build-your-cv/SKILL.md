# Build your CV

A CV is a short, honest summary of who you are and what you can do. Its only job
is to get you an interview. Keep it to **one or two pages**.

## What to put on it, in order

1. **Your name and how to reach you** — phone number and an email address you
   check. A home area or town is enough; you do not need a full street address.
2. **A short summary** — two or three lines: who you are, your main skill, and
   what work you are looking for. Rewrite this to match each job.
3. **Work experience**, most recent first — for each: job title, the place, the
   dates, and two or three lines on what you actually did. Use plain verbs:
   *drove, sold, fixed, cooked, looked after, built*.
4. **Education and training** — matric, any certificate, any short course. Say
   the year. If you did not finish matric, list the highest grade you passed and
   any skill you have instead.
5. **Skills** — real ones: a driver's licence and its code, languages you speak,
   machines or tools you can use, computer skills.
6. **References** — two people who will speak well of you (a past boss, a
   teacher, a community leader), with their phone numbers. Ask them first.

## Keep it safe and honest

- **Do not put your ID number, bank details, or a photo** unless the employer
  specifically asks — a CV gets passed around and those can be misused.
- **Never lie.** A made-up job or qualification is found in the interview or
  after, and it costs you the job. It is fine to be new to work — say what you
  are willing to learn.
- Check for spelling mistakes. Ask someone to read it.

## Common mistakes

- Sending the same CV for every job. Tailor the summary each time.
- Making it too long. Nobody reads five pages.
- No dates, so the reader cannot follow your story.
- A joke email address. Make a plain one with your name.

*Tell me the job you are applying for and a bit about your experience, and I will
help you write each part.*
